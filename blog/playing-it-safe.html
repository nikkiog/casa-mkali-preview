Blanding, not branding: the most expensive mistake in a crowded market is being impossible to disagree with.

july 2026 · 4 min

Open your phone. Find the explore page for any wellness, beauty, or fitness brand you don't already follow. Same warm neutral palette, same minimalist sans-serif, same "rooted in ritual" copy, same founder shot in soft window light. Same oat milk, same linen. Same five words in the bio that could belong to any of them.

We call this blanding, not branding, and it's one of the most expensive mistakes a brand can make, because most brands don't know they're doing it. Someone gets hired to build the brand, researches the category, sees what's performing, and builds toward it. The result is credible. It's also indistinguishable from five others in the same space, and when that happens the only lever left is cost. You cannot charge a premium for sameness.

The most common positioning problem we see isn't a brand saying the wrong thing. It's a brand that has said nothing: the right aesthetic, the right language, the right references, assembled into something no one disagrees with and no one remembers. Playing it safe feels like risk management. In a crowded market it's the riskiest move there is.

The brands actually cutting through are not the prettiest or the most produced. They're the most specific. They have a point of view that makes some people uncomfortable and other people feel like they finally found their people. They attract and they repel, and that's what makes them sticky.

Specificity is the one thing you cannot outsource, cannot replicate with AI, a bigger budget, or a better agency. It has to come from something true about what the brand believes, who it's for, and what it refuses to be. The question we start with before touching any creative: what is true about your brand that only you could say?

So: if your brand disappeared tomorrow, would your customer feel the loss? Or would they just find the next thing that looks like you?
